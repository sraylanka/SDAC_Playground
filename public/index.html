Equations and explanations for this playground.

01

Mₚ and %OS

Mₚ = %OS / 100
%OS = 100 Mₚ

Here Mₚ means the fractional step overshoot. A 20% limit means Mₚ ≤ 0.20; it does not mean Mₚ = 20.

Notation varies by lecturer and textbook. Some call percent overshoot “Mₚ”; always check the definition and units. A frequency-response resonant peak is a different quantity.

02

Overshoot → damping

Mₚ = e−πζ / √(1 − ζ²)
ζ = −ln Mₚ / √(π² + ln² Mₚ)

For the canonical underdamped response, more damping means less overshoot. An upper bound on Mₚ becomes a lower bound on ζ.

Use 0 < Mₚ < 1. Zero overshoot corresponds to ζ ≥ 1 for this model; the logarithm formula is evaluated by its limit.

03

Damping → phase margin

PM = tan⁻¹ [ 2ζ / √(√(1 + 4ζ⁴) − 2ζ²) ]

This relation is exact for L(s) = ωₙ² / [s(s + 2ζωₙ)] with unity negative feedback. PM increases with ζ, so the overshoot limit gives a PM lower bound for this model.

For a higher-order loop it is a design estimate. Zeros and other poles change the response. Verify the full closed-loop step response.

04

Settling time → pole location

Tₛ ≈ 4 / (ζωₙ) · ±2%
s = −ζωₙ ± jωₙ√(1 − ζ²)

For the usual underdamped approximation, Tₛ ≤ Tₛ,max implies ζωₙ ≥ 4/Tₛ,max. Poles farther left decay faster.

Use 3/(ζωₙ) for ±5%. These are textbook approximations; measured settling time can differ, especially near critical or overdamped conditions.

06

What phase margin means

PM = 180° + ∠L(jωgc)
|L(jωgc)| = 1

At unity loop gain, phase margin measures how much more phase lag reaches −180°. It describes a loop’s distance from that critical phase.

Positive PM alone does not establish stability for every loop. This explorer also checks closed-loop poles and reports every detected gain crossover.

Calculation notes & source references

The canonical response is analytic. Transfer-function roots use scaled complex iteration; frequency crossovers use logarithmic sampling and bisection. The entered system’s step response uses a state-space matrix exponential and 2,600 time samples. Overshoot and settling checks are numerical estimates over a finite horizon; narrow peaks or widely separated time scales need confirmation with MATLAB. The horizon is at least 12 slow-pole time constants for a stable loop.

Only exact zero coefficients identify origin factors. Common origin factors are removed before analysis; other common factors are retained. Transfer-function analysis cannot certify internal stability of a realization with hidden cancelled modes. Delays, discrete-time systems, MIMO systems, actuator limits, and nonlinear dynamics are outside this model.
